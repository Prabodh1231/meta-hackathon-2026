Today a citation is a pill that can't break across a line, so in justified text the words before it spread out (Electron on Windows can't hyphenate to help). A writes it as text in the page's font, muted, with a small mark after it: a line can break inside it, like a printed paper. B leaves out the page number (it's in the card). C is a superscript number per source, as in Word's endnotes. In every option, pointing at a citation shows its quote, and a flagged one keeps its symbol and colour (and a wavy underline in A and B), so colour isn't the only signal.
