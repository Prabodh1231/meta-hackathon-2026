Macro Financial Conditions and National Cryptocurrency Adoption

Background, the gap and how the reviewed studies measured each variable cite your review matrices and are checked against them. Everything from the research questions on is proposed for this study, so it cites no one.

Background

Bhimani et al. 2022 regress cryptocurrency adoption on ten development indicators across 137 countries and find only education, Gini index and network readiness index were statistically significant. Bhimani et al. 2022 Parino et al. 2018 correlate Bitcoin adoption with GDP per capita, freedom of trade and internet penetration for 72 countries in 2012–2014 using IP addresses and client downloads as proxies. Parino et al. 2018

Saiedi et al. 2021 use random-effects Tobit regression on Bitnode and CoinMap data for 137 countries from 2014 to 2018 and find rule of law is more significant in explaining bitnode intensity at 0.1% with greater coefficient magnitude. Saiedi et al. 2021

The gap

Cross-country indices dominate but geographic coverage is uneven. Bhimani et al. 2022 analyse 137 countries after approximating missing numbers by averaging neighbouring countries. Bhimani et al. 2022 Alonso et al. 2021 analyse 133 countries after excluding 11 for missing human capital index data. Alonso et al. 2021

Findings on macro drivers contradict each other. Bhimani et al. 2022 find only education, Gini and network readiness significant when ten indicators are tested together. Saiedi et al. 2021 find rule of law more significant at 0.1% for bitnode intensity. Saiedi et al. 2021 partly supported

Research questions

  1. Do macro-national development and financial conditions explain variation in national cryptocurrency adoption across countries?
  2. Which development and financial indicators remain associated with adoption when competing explanations are tested together?

Variables

Design

Cross-sectional secondary-data design. No primary data collection, no panel data, no proxy creation. Single cross-section for most recent year with complete public data.

Sample frame: all sovereign countries with complete data for dependent composite and the core independent set in that year. Target N = 120 to 140 after listwise deletion. Exclusion rule stated explicitly. No imputation by neighbor averaging, no interpolation, no English-only filtering.

Estimation: OLS with heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors as baseline. Check VIF <5 for multicollinearity. Alternative specification with standardized beta coefficients to compare indicator strength. Robustness: replace composite dependent with Chainalysis score alone.

Data

Sources: Chainalysis Global Crypto Adoption Index public report table, CoinATMRadar country counts, Bitnodes.io country snapshot, UNDP HDR, World Bank WDI 2024 vintage, Worldwide Governance Indicators 2024 update, IMF Financial Development Database, Portulans Network Readiness Index 2023 or 2024 report, Heritage Economic Freedom 2024, ITU statistics.

Data preparation: merge by ISO3 code. Keep only countries with non-missing dependent composite and all Block 1 and 2 indicators. Countries missing any variable are dropped for that model and reported in exclusion table with reason.

Analysis

Step 1 descriptive: mean, sd, min, max for each variable, N per variable, correlation matrix (Pearson and Spearman), distribution plots for adoption composite.

Step 2 bivariate: OLS regression of adoption composite on each independent indicator separately with robust SE, report beta, 95% CI, p-value, R2.

Step 3 blocks: hierarchical OLS adding Block 1, then Block 2, then Block 3 to test change in R2 and coefficient stability.
